# Moodboard brief

Two boards, one per pet, made in **[PureRef](https://www.pureref.com)** (free; a canvas you drag images onto). They steer every modelling decision, so the time spent here pays back many times.

**Where to keep them:** save each board as `art/moodboards/sausage-dog.pur` and `art/moodboards/husky.pur`, and export a PNG of each (PureRef: *Save → Export Scene*). That folder is **gitignored**: the boards are made of other people's images, so they stay off the public repo.

## Ground rules

- **Aim for 20–40 images per board.** Fewer reads as a guess; many more turns into noise.
- **Reference qualities, not characters.** Collect what you love *about* things (an ear shape, a smug expression, a fabric texture). The pets will be original designs, not lookalikes of an existing toy or cartoon.
- **The chosen style is cel-shaded with ink outlines.** Photos are great for shape and personality, but include stylised references (cartoons, illustrations, toy design) for how those qualities translate into flat colour and line.
- **The screen is tiny (320×240).** Every few images, zoom the PureRef canvas out until each image is thumbnail-sized. If a shape or expression still reads, keep it. Detail that vanishes doesn't matter.
- **Annotate.** PureRef lets you add text notes (right-click → *Text*). A two-word note ("these ears!", "too realistic") is worth more than three extra images.

## Lay each board out in four zones

Drag images into four rough areas, left to right. **Zones 2 and 3 should share images between the boards**, so the two pets feel like they live in the same world.

### 1. Shape & proportion (per pet)
How the body is built, as a **baby** first (that's what's needed first), then a few for teen/adult.
- Plush toys and baby animals: big head, short limbs, rounded everything.
- Silhouettes: could you recognise it from its shadow alone?
- **Sausage dog:** the long low body even as a pup; ear length and floppiness; how short legs can get before it's a slug.
- **Husky:** chunky fluff silhouette; ear shape (pointy but soft?); tail curl; the face mask pattern.

### 2. Material & colour (shared)
- Fabric and plush textures: fleece, velvet, knit, felt. What "soft" looks like.
- Palettes you love: warm/cosy vs fresh/bright. Pick 2–3 candidate palettes per pet.
- Cel-shaded examples where the shadow colour is a different *hue*, not just darker (that's what makes it look painted).
- Backgrounds: cosy rooms, dog beds, snowy windows. Ideas for each pet's home.

### 3. Faces & expressions (shared)
The single most important zone: personality lives here.
- Eyes: dot eyes, bead eyes, big glossy eyes, half-lidded. Which say "plush" and which say "cartoon"?
- Expressions with very few lines: smug, sulking, delighted, guilty, dizzy, sleepy.
- Deadpan faces that are funny *because* they barely move.
- Look for eyebrows: a single raised brow does a lot of work for a cheeky character.

### 4. Personality & humour (per pet)
How each pet *behaves*, which drives poses and animation.
- **Sausage dog (cheeky):** smug, scheming, food-obsessed, dramatic. Stills of characters mid-mischief; poses that say "I didn't do it".
- **Husky (boisterous):** loud, bouncy, chaotic, affectionate. Huskies "talking", zoomies, tongue-out joy, crashing into things.
- Comics and cartoon stills where a pose alone gets the joke across.
